# Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) - Hazardous Energy Control

## Purpose and Scope
This procedure controls hazardous energy (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, mechanical, thermal, gravity and stored energy) during servicing and maintenance of all plant equipment, including the HP-200 hydraulic press, VMC-500 machining centers, CV-3 conveyor line and AC-75 air compressor. LOTO is mandatory whenever any part of the body enters a point of operation, a guard is removed, or a safety device is bypassed.

## Authorized and Affected Employees
- Only **authorized employees** who have completed LOTO training (renewed every 12 months) may apply locks.
- Each authorized employee uses a personal **red lock** with a single key and a tag showing name, department, date and reason.
- Affected employees (operators in the area) must be notified before shutdown and after the equipment is returned to service.
- Supervisors may not remove another employee's lock. Abandoned-lock removal requires the EHS Manager and the procedure in section "Lock Removal by Others".

## Energy Isolation Steps
1. Prepare: review the machine-specific energy control placard posted on the equipment. Identify every energy source.
2. Notify all affected employees that the equipment will be shut down and locked out.
3. Shut down the equipment using normal stopping controls.
4. Isolate all energy sources at the disconnects / valves listed on the placard.
5. Apply your personal lock and tag to each isolation point. For group lockout, use the group lock box; every worker places a personal lock on the box.
6. Release or restrain stored energy: bleed hydraulic accumulators to 0 psi, vent pneumatic lines, block raised rams or platens with the rated safety blocks, and allow thermal components to cool below 50 C (122 F).
7. **Verify zero energy state ("try-out")**: attempt to start the machine with the normal controls, check voltage with a CAT III rated meter (test the meter on a known live source before and after), and confirm gauges read zero. Return controls to OFF after the try-out.

## Restoring Equipment to Service
1. Remove tools and materials; reinstall all guards.
2. Confirm all employees are clear of the machine.
3. Each authorized employee removes only their own lock and tag.
4. Notify affected employees that the equipment is being restarted.

## Lock Removal by Others
If an employee leaves the site with a lock applied, the EHS Manager must (a) verify the employee is not on site, (b) attempt to contact the employee, (c) document the removal on form EHS-114, and (d) inform the employee before their next shift.

## Shift Change
Locks are transferred lock-on-lock: the incoming worker applies their lock before the outgoing worker removes theirs. Equipment must never be left without a lock during transfer.

## Escalation
Any LOTO deviation or near miss must be reported to the EHS Manager (ext. 4400) before the end of the shift.
