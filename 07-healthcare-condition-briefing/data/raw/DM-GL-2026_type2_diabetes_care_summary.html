Type 2 Diabetes: Standards of Care Summary 2026

Document ID: DM-GL-2026 | Revision: 2 | Classification: Public | Owner: Synthetic Guideline Consortium

This synthetic summary condenses the current standard of care for adults with type 2 diabetes. It is written for a document-AI demonstration and is not clinical guidance.

1. Diagnosis and Targets

Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed with an A1C of 6.5% or higher, a fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, or a 2-hour glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test. An A1C target below 7% is appropriate for many non-pregnant adults; less stringent targets (below 8%) suit people with limited life expectancy or frequent hypoglycemia.

2. First-Line Management

Lifestyle therapy - medical nutrition therapy, at least 150 minutes per week of physical activity and diabetes self-management education - is the foundation. Metformin remains a common first-line agent when there are no compelling cardiorenal indications.

3. Cardiorenal Risk-Based Therapy

For people with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor or a GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven benefit is recommended independent of A1C and of metformin use. Heart failure and CKD favor an SGLT2 inhibitor; weight management goals favor a GLP-1 receptor agonist or a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist.

4. Technology and Monitoring

Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) is recommended for adults on insulin and is increasingly used for people on non-insulin therapy. Time in range (70-180 mg/dL) above 70% is the common CGM target. A1C is checked at least twice a year when at target.

5. Emerging Therapies

Pipeline activity centers on oral and multi-agonist incretin therapies, once-weekly basal insulin, glucokinase activators, long-wear CGM devices and beta-cell replacement. Remission through structured weight loss is an expanding care-delivery model. See the synthetic registry for programs and sponsors.

6. Quality Measures

  • A1C control below 8%: target 70% of the diabetes panel or higher.
  • Annual kidney health evaluation (eGFR and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio): target 80% or higher.
  • Statin therapy for people aged 40-75: target 85% or higher.
