# Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF): Guideline Summary 2026

Summary. This synthetic summary condenses the current standard of care for adults with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF): diagnosis, guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), device therapy, advanced care, emerging therapies and quality measures. It was written to demonstrate document extraction and is not clinical guidance.

### 1. Definition and Epidemiology

HFrEF is heart failure with a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 40% or lower. It accounts for roughly half of heart failure cases in this summary's reference population. Patients with an LVEF between 41% and 49% are classified as mildly reduced ejection fraction and are managed separately.

Five-year mortality after a first heart failure hospitalization remains close to 50% in the reference registry, and 30-day readmission after discharge averages 21%, which makes HFrEF a priority for value-based care programs.

### 2. Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines symptoms, natriuretic peptides and imaging. An NT-proBNP above 125 pg/mL in the outpatient setting supports the diagnosis; transthoracic echocardiography confirms the LVEF and identifies structural causes.

Initial work-up includes ECG, renal function, potassium, iron studies and screening for ischemic heart disease.

Iron deficiency (ferritin below 100 ng/mL, or 100-299 ng/mL with transferrin saturation below 20%) should be corrected.

## 3. Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT)

The foundation of HFrEF care is four drug classes started together rather than sequentially: an angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI), an evidence-based beta-blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) and an SGLT2 inhibitor. Each class independently reduces mortality or hospitalization.

Rapid sequencing - all four pillars within six weeks and dose titration every two weeks - is preferred over waiting for target doses of one drug before adding the next. Table 1 lists recommended agents and target doses.

## 4. Device Therapy and Advanced Care

Device decisions follow at least three months of optimized GDMT, because LVEF often improves with therapy.

Patients who remain in NYHA class IV despite therapy (Stage D) should be referred early to an advanced heart failure program. Palliative care is integrated alongside life-prolonging treatment, not only at the end of life.

## 5. Emerging Therapies

Development is concentrated in four areas: cardiac myosin activation to improve contractility, nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists with lower hyperkalemia risk, gene therapy targeting calcium handling (SERCA2a), and device-based monitoring that detects congestion before symptoms.

Digital and care-delivery programs that close the GDMT gap are a fast-growing category, because fewer than one in four eligible patients receives all four pillars at target dose.
